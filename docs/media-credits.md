**Source:** the V1.0 tactile dataset associated with *Embedded real-time objects

## Burgers

The Burgers preview redraws a released trajectory from **Neural Operator Discovery Burgers Dataset**, by **Zituo Chen**, [Zenodo record 20372988 / DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20372988](https://zenodo.org/records/20372988), released under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The spatial curve and space–time field use the `RdBu_r` display colormap and fixed signed color limits. All 101 stored states retain their order and numerical values; simulation time 0–5 seconds is displayed at 10 frames/s. The poster selects frame 50; the thumbnail renders the full space–time field with nearest-neighbor resizing. The included thumbnail and its SVG wrapper are shared under CC BY 4.0 with this attribution and change notice. These are numerical-data views, not native camera frames or learned predictions.

## FitzHugh–Nagumo

The FHN model and display palette follow the DR2D generator in **Neural Operator Discovery Code**, by **Zituo Chen**, [Zenodo record 20406332 / DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20406332](https://zenodo.org/records/20406332). This separate **software** record is released under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The displayed trajectory was newly generated by a Python reimplementation of the released equations, periodic stencil and RK4 integration (seed 42, k = 0.03, beta = 0.20). It is a visualization-only trajectory, not an official released FHN trajectory or a sample from the Burgers data record. Both fields use the upstream generator's `summer` colormap, fixed shared signed color limits and 101 stored states over simulation time 0–10 seconds, displayed at 10 frames/s. Poster and thumbnail use the final state. The code-record license is cited for the generator source; the Burgers data license is not used as the licensing basis for the generated trajectory.

## Overcooked

Rendering uses work by the **JaxMARL contributors** (Alexander Rutherford et al.): the [Overcooked V2 visualizer](https://github.com/FLAIROx/JaxMARL/blob/main/jaxmarl/viz/overcooked_v2_visualizer.py) and [grid-rendering routines](https://github.com/FLAIROx/JaxMARL/blob/main/jaxmarl/viz/grid_rendering_v2.py). The repository, originally FLAIROx/JaxMARL and now redirected to bold-lab-ai/JaxMARL, distributes the software under [Apache License 2.0](https://github.com/FLAIROx/JaxMARL/blob/main/LICENSE). Imports were remapped to local environment constants; raster drawing was retained and the GUI was unused. Two UtilityGreedy controllers produce the illustrative rollout. The complete 121-frame source GIF was re-encoded with nearest-neighbor resizing and an illustrative-demonstration label, retaining layout, frame order and 120 ms per frame. Poster and thumbnail select or crop a frame. This is not a trained-policy result; the renderer's software license is not asserted as a universal license for generated footage.

## Pendulum

The dynamics use the released **CaDM** environment by **Kimin Lee, Younggyo Seo, Seunghyun Lee, Honglak Lee and Jinwoo Shin**, *Context-aware Dynamics Model for Generalization in Model-Based Reinforcement Learning* (ICML 2020), from the [official repository](https://github.com/younggyoseo/CaDM). The display reproduces the geometry of [OpenAI Gym 0.16 Pendulum](https://github.com/openai/gym/blob/0.16.0/gym/envs/classic_control/pendulum.py); Gym is copyright 2016 OpenAI and distributed under the [MIT License](https://github.com/openai/gym/blob/0.16.0/LICENSE.md). This is a new fixed-input torque demonstration using unchanged CaDM reset/step and normalization methods. The geometry and colors were redrawn with Pillow, with torque and response overlays, rather than captured as native Gym pixels. The 161 frames are displayed at 20 frames/s, with a selected poster and cropped thumbnail. Gym's license is not extended to CaDM; the linked CaDM repository provides no top-level license, and no separate CaDM licensing grant is claimed here.

## Other project-generated previews

D-Clean redraws stored numerical states and actual forces at half physical speed with force/drag overlays. PokeWorld replays recorded method-figure histories at quarter speed with normalized-action arrows and trajectory overlays. SpringWorld and Swimmer use new illustrative inputs in the project's simulation models, with force/torque annotations, selected posters and cropped thumbnails. They add no evaluation results. SpringWorld and Swimmer use **MuJoCo**, whose software is distributed under [Apache License 2.0](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/blob/3.3.5/LICENSE); this software acknowledgment does not assign that license to every generated image.
